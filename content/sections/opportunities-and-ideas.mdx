This review identified problems to fix. But there's a larger opportunity: your platform can do much more to turn portfolio visibility into client relationships and pipeline.

## The opportunity: From portfolio to sales engine

Thunderhawk's existing client roster is genuinely impressive — Hasbro, EA Sports, Lego, The North Face, Aldi. These aren't small-time projects. Yet your website largely shows them in a static portfolio. That's leaving money on the table.

Your platform could be:
- **A client collaboration hub** — where approved clients log in to review work in progress, share feedback and approve assets
- **A case study generator** — automatically creating polished public pages from client collaboration data, complete with before/after, process, results
- **A conversation starter** — striking, shareable interactive experiences that get attention on social media and in press coverage
- **A lead magnet** — free resources (animation presets, design tools, frameworks) that attract designers and creative professionals to Thunderhawk's orbit

This transforms the website from "here's what we've done" to "here's what we can do for you — and here's proof of impact."

## Idea 1: Private client portal with public case study generation

### What this does

Clients get a branded, private space where they can:
- Review work in progress (3D renders, animations, design variations)
- Provide structured feedback (approvals, revision requests, comments)
- Download approved assets
- See a timeline of the project and decision history

The system automatically extracts structured data — timeline, revisions, stakeholder approvals, final assets — and generates a polished public case study page on your marketing site.

### Why it matters

**For Thunderhawk:**
- Reduces email chains and revision chaos
- Creates documented proof of process (showing complex work, not just output)
- Generates fresh content automatically without team overhead
- Demonstrates collaborative process to prospective clients

**For clients:**
- Professional, organised project experience
- Pride in their association with Thunderhawk (seeing their project elevate their own brand)
- Proof of what Thunderhawk delivered

**For prospects:**
- Case studies show real client problems and solutions, not generic portfolio fluff
- Seeing process builds confidence ("they're organised, they document everything")
- Social proof at scale

### Implementation approach

Start with one or two existing clients. Build a private portal (could be hosted separately or integrated into Webflow). Structure around:
- **Project brief** — client goals, constraints, deliverables
- **Timeline** — key milestones, revisions, approvals
- **Assets** — organised by type (renders, animations, comps, finals)
- **Feedback loop** — revision requests, approvals, sign-offs
- **Public export** — generated case study that goes live on your site

The public case study could include:
- Client testimonial (if they approve)
- Problem statement and approach
- Selected work-in-progress (showing your thinking)
- Final deliverables
- Metrics or results (if client shares them)
- Link to their work (with permission)

## Idea 2: Strike with interactive web experiences

### What this does

Create bold, experimental interactive web experiences that:
- Showcase Thunderhawk's animation and interaction design prowess
- Are genuinely shareable on social media and design communities
- Could be embedded in case studies or used as standalone thought leadership
- Demonstrate what's possible when creative direction meets technical excellence

Think less "polished portfolio piece" and more "wow, how did they do that?" — the kind of work that gets shared in design Slack channels and featured on design blogs.

### Why it matters

Your competitors are showing static work. You could be showing interactive experiences that only a studio with both creative and technical depth can pull off.

Examples:
- **Animated case study** — scroll through a project's evolution with choreographed animations revealing process
- **Interactive 3D scene explorer** — let people rotate, animate and interact with finished 3D assets
- **Generative design tool** — a simple tool that creates unique variations of a design pattern (e.g., "generate a unique Thunderhawk-style animation")
- **Motion typography showcase** — typography and text that moves in unexpected, beautiful ways (inspired by work like [kielm.com](https://www.kielm.com))

### Implementation approach

Pick one concept. Build it as a standalone experience (not attached to a client project). Make it:
- Fast (loads instantly, no bloat)
- Shareable (works well on social, has a clear URL)
- On-brand (uses Thunderhawk's visual language but pushes it)
- Documented (blog post explaining the technique or concept)

Post it on Twitter, Instagram, Design Twitter, Dribbble. Link it from your case studies. Use it in pitches to prospects. "This is the level of craft we bring."

## Idea 3: Free resources & community tools

### What this does

Offer free, genuinely useful resources that position Thunderhawk as a generous expert and funnel people into your orbit:

- **Animation preset packs** — GSAP animations, Spline interactions, or 3D model templates that designers can download and use
- **Design framework** — a Figma kit or CSS framework that encodes Thunderhawk's approach to motion, spacing, and interaction
- **Tool or utility** — something small but useful (e.g., a color animation tool, a 3D asset organiser, a timing calculator for animations)
- **Tutorial or guide** — free video/written guide on a technique people care about (e.g., "how to optimise 3D assets for web" or "building interactive 3D scenes without code")

### Why it matters

**Builds audience:**
- People download your preset → sign up for your email list
- They use your tool → refer friends → word of mouth
- People watch your tutorial → subscribe to your content

**Establishes expertise:**
- Free work demonstrates what you know
- It's generous without being wasteful (you're reusing existing work)
- It's the kind of thing that gets shared in professional communities

**Gets press:**
- "Thunderhawk releases free animation presets for Spline" is a small news peg
- Design blogs pick it up
- It's worth a social media push

### Implementation approach

Start with one resource. Make it genuinely useful (not a thin lead magnet). Examples:
- Extract Spline animation patterns you've built for clients → package as preset pack
- Document your CSS animation approach → release as framework with examples
- Record one good tutorial on something you do well → post on YouTube

Gate it lightly (name + email for download) to build your email list. Promote on social and design communities.

## Idea 4: More aggressive calls-to-action

### What this does

Your site should have more reasons for people to engage:

- **"Get inspiration" signup** — weekly email of Thunderhawk's latest work and thinking
- **"Let's talk"** — clear CTA on every page for prospect inquiries (currently buried)
- **"Download [resource]"** — specific offers (presets, guide, framework) with email capture
- **"See behind-the-scenes"** — process videos, time-lapses, or behind-the-scenes content on Instagram Stories/TikTok
- **"Join our community"** — Discord or Slack community for animators/designers interested in 3D and web motion

### Why it matters

Visitors come to your site, see cool work, and leave. You want multiple points where they can say "yes, I want more of this."

Each CTA is also data: who's interested in presets vs. process videos vs. client work tells you what resonates.

### Implementation approach

Add CTAs strategically:
- After impressive case study → "Like what you see? Let's talk"
- In footer → "Weekly inspiration" signup
- On resource pages → "Download [thing]"
- On Instagram profile → Link to latest interactive experience or tutorial

Track which CTAs convert. Double down on what works.

## Idea 5: Branding and visual direction

### What this does

Your visual identity (colours, typography, layout) feels borrowed from a template. It should be distinctly Thunderhawk.

Opportunities:
- **Refresh the colour palette** — move beyond safe greys and reds into something more distinctive
- **Own your typography** — custom type or distinctive type pairing that becomes recognisable
- **Motion as brand** — animations and interactions that are instantly Thunderhawk (consistent, recognisable, surprising)
- **Visual system** — clear rules for how layouts, cards, sections and components work (not just "follow Webflow defaults")

### Why it matters

When prospects see Thunderhawk's site, they should immediately sense "these people have a point of view." Right now it reads as competent but generic.

Your visual identity should feel like an extension of your creative work, not separate from it.

### Implementation approach

This deserves a design conversation separate from the platform audit. But the key is: **your visual system should showcase your creative thinking, not hide it.**

If your site is about 3D and animation, your design should celebrate those things. Motion should feel intentional and beautiful, not just "loading state." Colours should be purposeful, not safe.

## Prioritisation

These ideas range from "quick wins" to "significant projects":

**Quick wins (weeks, not months):**
- More aggressive CTAs across the site
- Free resource pack (extract + package existing work)
- Blog/social content about your process

**Medium term (1-2 months):**
- Interactive web experience (pick one concept, build it)
- Tutorial or guide (video or written)
- Visual identity refresh (colours, typography)

**Longer term (2-3 months):**
- Client portal + case study generation system
- Ongoing content/community strategy

## Next steps

The question isn't "can we do these things?" — you can. The question is "which of these would move the needle most for Thunderhawk?"

That's worth a conversation with your team:
- What would get the most traction with your audience?
- Which ideas align with how you want to position the studio?
- What does your team have capacity to maintain long-term?

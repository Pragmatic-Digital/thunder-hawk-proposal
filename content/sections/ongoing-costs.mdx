The build estimates cover the delivery work described in this proposal, but the initial development cost is only one part of the commercial decision.

Each option has a different ongoing cost profile across platform and commercial licences, hosting and infrastructure, search and paid applications, maintenance and support, and any applicable payment or transaction charges.

These costs should be considered alongside the initial investment when comparing the available options.

## Recommended support

Whichever approach [Client Name] chooses, we recommend retaining an ongoing support arrangement covering essential platform maintenance and operational support, with planned improvements managed separately.

The maintenance element would cover the agreed responsibilities for software updates, monitoring, backups and the health of the custom integrations. Operational support would provide [Client Name] with a clear route for reporting problems, assessing their impact and coordinating a response.

For a headless rebuild, Pragmatic's support responsibilities would extend to the application itself, its hosting environment and infrastructure, monitoring and backups, alongside the [Core System] integration and bespoke functionality.

For a platform enhancement approach, we would continue to support the existing application, infrastructure and integrations, together with any additional services introduced as part of the improvement work.

The final support arrangement will define the included services, support hours, incident priorities and escalation process. Response targets will be distinguished from resolution times, particularly where resolving an issue requires input from [Core System] or another third party.

Following launch, we recommend reviewing actual support demand once the platform has been operating normally. This will allow the ongoing maintenance allowance to reflect the support [Client Name] genuinely requires rather than simply carrying the existing arrangement forward unchanged.

## Recurring costs by option

### Headless rebuild

Ongoing costs would include application hosting and infrastructure, monitoring and backups, search services, maintenance and support, together with any paid third-party services selected for the implementation.

A headless rebuild does not introduce a recurring software subscription. Unlike a SaaS platform, [Client Name] would not be paying a single platform fee that bundles the application, infrastructure and platform services together. This provides greater flexibility over the technical architecture, but the individual services required to operate the platform need to be allowed for separately.

### SaaS platform option

Ongoing costs would include the platform subscription, any paid applications, applicable payment and transaction charges, and the hosting and support required for bespoke applications and integrations.

Although the SaaS platform manages the core commerce functionality and its underlying infrastructure, this does not remove the requirement to maintain [Client Name]'s [Core System] integration, bespoke customer functionality or any other custom applications developed around the platform.

The platform subscription should therefore be considered alongside, rather than instead of, the ongoing technical support requirement.

### Existing platform enhancement

This option would retain the existing application, hosting, infrastructure and maintenance requirements.

Additional recurring costs may also be introduced where new services are required, for example external search or other third-party tools used to improve the experience.

Because the underlying platform remains in place, this route does not remove the existing requirement for application and infrastructure maintenance.

## Reviewing the retainer

### Current support arrangement

[Client Name] currently retains Pragmatic for 37.5 hours per month, charged at our standard rate of £700 per day / £3,500 per month.

This is currently allocated as:

| Area | Allowance |
|------|-----------|
| Platform maintenance | 7.5 hours |
| Platform development | 22.5 hours |
| Other projects | 7.5 hours |
| **Total** | **37.5 hours / £3,500 per month** |

We propose keeping the existing £700 day rate unchanged.

### Reduced support requirement with rebuild

One of the benefits we would expect from a full platform rebuild is a reduction in the amount of ongoing maintenance required simply to keep the portal stable and deal with issues created by the existing platform.

The new platform would be built around the current [Core System] integration, customer rules and agreed workflows rather than continuing to maintain and extend a platform that has evolved over a number of years. [Client Name]'s team would also have better tools for managing routine content and platform updates themselves.

For that reason, our proposed ongoing arrangement following a successful rebuild would reduce the dedicated platform maintenance allowance from 7.5 hours to 3.75 hours per month once the new platform has completed its post-launch support period and is operating normally.

The existing 22.5 hours of platform development and 7.5 hours for other projects can remain available, meaning [Client Name] continues to have development capacity for improvements rather than using as much of the retainer on routine maintenance.

This would give an ongoing allocation of:

| Area | Current | Proposed with rebuild |
|------|---------|----------------------|
| Platform maintenance | 7.5 hours | 3.75 hours |
| Platform development | 22.5 hours | 22.5 hours |
| Other projects | 7.5 hours | 7.5 hours |
| **Total** | **37.5 hours** | **33.75 hours** |
| **Monthly cost at existing £700/day rate** | **£3,500** | **£3,150** |

This represents a £350 monthly reduction, or £4,200 per year, while retaining the same development allowance and other project support.

Alternatively, [Client Name] could retain the existing £3,500 monthly commitment and use the released maintenance capacity for additional development and continuous improvement.

We would review the maintenance allowance with [Client Name] following launch to make sure it remains appropriate for the live platform. This reduction relates specifically to a full rebuild and does not include hosting, infrastructure, commercial licences or other third-party services, which are shown separately within the ongoing cost comparison.

The objective is to make the ongoing cost benefit of replacing Magento tangible, while maintaining enough support capacity for a business-critical platform.

## Platform dependency and future control

The choice between a headless rebuild and a managed SaaS platform is not only about the initial build or ongoing cost. It also affects how much control [Client Name] has over the platform, how future requirements can be delivered and how dependent the business becomes on a particular technology provider.

Both approaches are viable, but they represent different long-term operating models.

### Headless rebuild

A headless architecture provides a different model.

For [Client Name], we are proposing a bespoke, open-source based rebuild rather than a commercial hosted platform. This provides an extensible foundation that we can develop around [Client Name]'s operating model and bespoke requirements.

This gives [Client Name] greater flexibility over how the application is structured, extended and hosted, particularly where requirements are specific to [Client Name]'s customer relationships, pricing model or integration with [Core System].

It also provides more choice over how the platform is supported in future. The implementation can be maintained by Pragmatic or another appropriately experienced development team without the entire commerce application being tied to a single hosted platform.

That flexibility does not mean there are no dependencies or ongoing responsibilities. A custom implementation still requires hosting, maintenance, monitoring, documentation and appropriate technical expertise. The underlying framework is free to use and self-host under open-source licensing, so there is no commercial platform subscription included in our proposal. [Client Name] would still have ongoing costs for the infrastructure and services required to operate the application, together with Pragmatic's maintenance and support.

### Managed SaaS platform

A managed SaaS platform provides a mature, fully managed commerce solution, with the core service and underlying infrastructure operated and maintained by the vendor.

This removes a significant amount of responsibility for running the core commerce platform and gives [Client Name] access to the wider ecosystem. The trade-off is that the platform remains dependent on the vendor's service, commercial terms and supported methods of extending the platform.

For [Client Name], this is particularly relevant because the proposed portal goes beyond a standard ecommerce implementation.

Custom customer relationships, account-specific functionality and the [Core System] workflows would need to operate within the platform's capabilities or be delivered through bespoke applications and integrations around it.

This does not prevent those requirements from being delivered, but it means future changes may sometimes need to be approached in a way that fits the platform architecture rather than being designed entirely around [Client Name]'s preferred process.

If [Client Name] chose to move away from the SaaS platform in future, this would require a migration project. Functionality developed specifically around the platform would also need to be reviewed and, where necessary, rebuilt for the replacement system.

### What this means for [Client Name]

The distinction is therefore less about one approach being 'open' and another being 'closed', and more about the level of control [Client Name] wants over a business-critical system.

A managed platform provides the convenience and operational benefits of vendor-supported service, but with greater dependency on that vendor's ecosystem and commercial model.

A headless rebuild places more responsibility around the application and its operation with the implementation team, but provides greater flexibility to shape the platform around [Client Name] and more choice over how it is hosted, developed and supported in future.

Given the level of bespoke customer functionality and [Core System] integration required for this project, we believe that additional flexibility is an important advantage of the headless approach and is one of the reasons it is our recommended option.

### Licensing and commercial terms

The proposed solution is based on open-source frameworks.

These are free to use and self-host under standard open-source licensing. The proposal does not assume any commercial platform subscription.

This means there is no recurring software subscription included in the proposed solution. Ongoing costs will instead relate to the infrastructure and services used to run the application, including hosting, database, storage, monitoring, backups and any separately selected third-party services, together with Pragmatic's maintenance and support.

The implementation should be designed so [Client Name]'s bespoke functionality is properly separated and documented, supporting a cleaner upgrade path while retaining the flexibility that is one of the main reasons for recommending a custom rebuild approach.
